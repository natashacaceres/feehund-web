How to tell if a card machine comparison is reliable

Many comparison sites get paid if you sign up.

When a business wants to change card machine, the usual thing is to search Google for ‘best card machine for the self-employed’ or ‘card machine comparison’. Lists appear with medals, stars and ‘Go to offer’ buttons, and it looks as if someone has already done the work of comparing.

Often that is not the case. Behind many of these lists there is a commission that the website earns if you sign up through its link. It is legal and common on the internet, but it is almost never explained next to the list, which is where the decision is made.

Here we explain how these comparisons work and what to look at to know whether you can trust one.

What you should know before trusting a comparison

How does a card machine comparison site make money?

Most are funded by affiliate links. Every time a business signs up for a card machine through one of their buttons, the card machine company pays the website a commission. Others earn money from advertising or from featuring a brand.

Some mention it in one line at the bottom of the page. Others present themselves as ‘independent’ and, on the same page, admit that they carry sponsored links. And some websites say openly that they look for deals with companies in the sector to earn money when you sign up.

Why do the same brands almost always appear?

Because many card machine companies pay for each new customer who comes to them. In the United Kingdom there are programmes that pay between 30 and 50 pounds for each business referred, and in Spain there are also companies that offer commissions to anyone who brings them customers.

That is why the same four or five brands keep appearing in Google's comparisons. Banks, which publish little pricing, appear rarely or with a simple ‘as per contract’. A list of three or four options is not the whole market.

How much is normal to pay?

According to the Banco de España (Bank of Spain), the average fee banks charge for each card payment has been 0,38 % since 2022. It is an average for all retail, including large chains, so a small business usually pays a little more.

Even so, the gap with the 1,25 % or 1,49 % of many of the most recommended rates online is large. With 5.000 € a month in card payments, each percentage point of fees is 50 € a month.

Why does almost nobody compare?

In the United Kingdom, the payments regulator studied this market in 2021 and concluded that it does not work well for small businesses. They almost never compare or switch provider, and long-standing customers pay more on average than new ones.

The same study found that almost nine in ten businesses that negotiated with their provider got a better price. Comparing and asking works.

Five signs that a comparison is reliable

  1. It says how it makes money, and says it next to the list, not hidden at the bottom of the page.
  2. It compares many options. Three or four brands are not the whole market.
  3. It includes banks. If none appear, half the picture is missing.
  4. It explains how it ranks the results and how recent the pricing is.
  5. It does not put a ‘Go to offer’ button on every option. That button is usually the one that pays.
