My insurance has gone up. Can I reverse the direct debit?

You can, but reversing the direct debit does not cancel the insurance. Check these three dates first.

Can I reverse the insurance direct debit?

Yes. If you pay by direct debit, you have eight weeks from the charge to ask your bank to refund it, with no explanation needed. But reversing the direct debit does not cancel the insurance. The contract continues and the insurer can claim what you owe.

What happens if I reverse it?

One month after the expiry date you are left without cover. If during that time you have a car accident or a leak at home, the insurance does not pay. The insurer can claim the premium for the current year, and some put the debt on a register of defaulters.

If it is your car insurance, you cannot drive without cover. The fine for driving without insurance ranges from 601 to 3.005 €.

Did they have to tell me about the increase?

Yes. The insurer must tell you about any change to the contract at least two months before the expiry date. That is what article 22 of the Insurance Contract Act says.

If they told you later or did not tell you, complain first to the insurer's customer service department. If they do not reply or do not agree with you, complain to the Servicio de Reclamaciones de la Dirección General de Seguros (Complaints Service of the Directorate-General for Insurance).

How do I say I don't want to renew?

In writing and at least one month before the expiry date. Make sure there is proof. A burofax, an email with proof of receipt or the customer area with a confirmation will do. Your policy must state clearly how and by when you can do it.

Before you send it, have the new insurance in place from the same day as the expiry date, especially if it is your car insurance.

What if the date has already passed?

This year the insurance renews at the new price. You can call and ask them to lower it, and having an offer from another company helps. Note the date for next year.

Why has it gone up?

Almost all insurance goes up. Car insurance rose by 8 % on average in 2025, according to Kelisto, and home insurance by 5,8 % in the first half of 2026, according to ICEA. Insurers put it down to more expensive repairs and more claims. If you have made claims, they count too.

Can I trust insurance comparison sites?

Many get paid by the insurer when you switch insurance through them. Their advice may be good, but bear it in mind. We explain it in How to tell if a comparison is reliable.
