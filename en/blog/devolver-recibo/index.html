Can I reverse a direct debit my bank has charged me?

Yes, and it's legal. You have eight weeks if you authorised it and thirteen months if you didn't. We explain how to do it, what happens next and when it's not a good idea.

Electricity, your mobile, the gym or insurance are charged automatically every month. They are direct debits: the company has your IBAN and asks your bank for the money. What few people know is that you can ask your bank to give it back to you.

Is it legal to reverse a direct debit?

Yes. It is a right given to you by Real Decreto-ley 19/2018 on payment services, which brings a European law into Spain. It is not a trick or a favour from the bank. If you are within the time limit, the bank cannot refuse (article 49).

How long do I have?

  • Eight weeks from the charge, if you authorised it. For example, your gym fee or your mobile bill. You don't have to give any explanation (articles 48 and 49).
  • Thirteen months from the charge, if you didn't authorise it. A company you don't know or something you never signed up for. Report it as soon as you see it: the law asks you to do so without delay (article 43).

The bank has ten working days to give you the money back or explain why not. It pays it back with the same date as the charge, so you lose nothing along the way (articles 48 and 49).

How do I do it?

  1. Find the direct debit in your bank's app or website. In most of them, when you tap the transaction you'll see «Return direct debit». If not, call or go to your branch.
  2. If you didn't authorise it, say so. It is not the same as a normal return and it gives you more time.
  3. Keep the confirmation with the date.

If I reverse the direct debit, have I cancelled?

No. Reversing the direct debit undoes the payment, not what you owe. The contract continues and the company can charge you again or claim it. Some add charges for the returned direct debit. If you don't pay, they can cut off the service or put the debt on a register of defaulters.

If you want to leave, cancel first and keep the proof. To stop them charging you again, also ask your bank to cancel the direct debit mandate. Do it in writing and before the end of the working day before the charge. If it arrives late, it applies to the following direct debits, according to the Banco de España (Bank of Spain).

So what is it for?

  • To stop fraud. If someone uses your IBAN without permission, you get the money back.
  • For a charge that doesn't add up. A duplicate direct debit, one higher than usual or one that arrives after you cancelled. You reverse it and complain with the money already back in your account.
  • To get some breathing room in a tight month. The money comes back, but you still owe it. It buys you time, not savings.

When is it not a good idea?

  • Taxes, the town council and self-employed contributions. There may be surcharges and you would lose discounts.
  • Insurance. You could be left without cover. We explain it in My insurance has gone up. Can I reverse the direct debit?
  • If you are self-employed or a business. Your contract with the bank can take away the eight weeks (article 34). Check it first.

What if the bank says no?

Complain in writing to its customer service department. For direct debits it has 15 working days to reply. If it doesn't, or doesn't agree with you, complain to the Banco de España.

Does it work for card payments?

Not in the same way. This is for direct debits charged to your IBAN. If you see a card payment you didn't make, tell your bank as soon as possible: you also have up to thirteen months. But a card payment you did make can't be returned like this. You claim it from the shop.
