How to read your card machine's monthly statement

Six figures, one by one. Tick them off as you find them.

The percentage in your contract is not what you pay. What you really pay is in your card machine's monthly statement, a document hardly anyone looks at. Have yours to hand and go step by step.

Where is the statement?

In your bank's online business banking, in the card machine, TPV or merchant section. Depending on the bank it is called extracto, liquidación or resumen de operaciones. If you can't find it, ask your branch. Some banks charge to send it, and CaixaBank, for example, lists 2 € per statement on request in its price list. European law requires your bank to show you the fee for each payment separately.

0 of 6 found

  1. 1✓

    What you sold by card

    What it is

    The total your customers paid you by card or phone during the month, before fees. If there are refunds, subtract them.

    What it may be called

    • Importe de operaciones (transaction amount)
    • Facturación (turnover)
    • Importe bruto (gross amount)
    • Ventas (sales)

  2. 2✓

    How many payments you took

    What it is

    How many times you took a card payment during the month. It matters because many rates charge a fixed amount on each payment, and on small payments that amount weighs a lot.

    What it may be called

    • Nº de operaciones (number of transactions)
    • Operaciones (transactions)
    • Transacciones (transactions)

  3. 3✓

    Which cards your customers used

    What it is

    Many statements split payments by card type, and each type can have a different fee. Business cards and cards from outside Europe usually cost more. If your statement doesn't split them, that's fine, move on to the next one.

    What it may be called

    • Desglose por tipo de tarjeta (breakdown by card type)
    • Débito y crédito (debit and credit)
    • Particular y empresa (personal and business)
    • Nacional, UE y fuera de la UE (domestic, EU and non-EU)
    • Visa, Mastercard, American Express, Bizum

  4. 4✓

    What the bank charged you for the payments

    What it is

    The fee for the month, in euros. It usually adds up the percentage and the fixed amount for each payment. It is the most important figure.

    What it may be called

    • Importe descuento (discount amount)
    • Descuento aplicado (discount applied)
    • Comisión (fee)
    • Tasa de descuento (discount rate)

  5. 5✓

    What reached your account

    What it is

    What you sold minus the fee. It helps you check step 4. If you can't find the fee, subtract what reached your account from what you sold.

    What it may be called

    • Importe abonado (amount credited)
    • Importe neto (net amount)
    • Total abono (total credit)
    • Liquidado (settled)

  6. 6✓

    What they charge you separately

    What it is

    The card machine rental, a monthly fee or a minimum turnover charge. They often don't appear on the statement, but as a separate charge on your account or on another invoice. Look for it in the month's transactions. Card payment fees carry no VAT, but the rental does. Note it down without VAT.

    What it may be called

    • Alquiler TPV (card machine rental)
    • Cuota mensual (monthly fee)
    • Comisión de mantenimiento (maintenance fee)
    • Mínimo de facturación (minimum turnover)
