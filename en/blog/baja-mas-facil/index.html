Is cancelling about to get easier?

Europe is proposing that if you can sign up online, you should also be able to cancel online. Here is what changes, what doesn't and when.

What has Europe proposed?

The European Commission has presented the Digital Fairness Act, a law to protect people who shop online. One of its measures is to make cancelling as easy as signing up.

What changes for me?

If a company lets you sign up online, it will also have to let you cancel online, with a clear button. Even if you signed up in a shop or over the phone.

A free trial will only be able to turn into a paid subscription if you agree.

And automatic renewals nobody asked for would come to an end.

From when?

Not yet. It is a proposal, and the European Parliament and the EU member states have to approve it. Experts expect it will not be in force before the end of 2028.

What if the company doesn't sell online?

Then the button will not be mandatory. Companies that only sign customers up in a shop or over the phone will still be able to ask you to call or write.

Is there any button already?

Yes, but only for backing out. Since June 2026, Europe has required online shops to offer a button to withdraw from a purchase during the first few days, usually 14. When the rule started to apply, Spain had not yet written it into its own law, according to the consumer association OCU. That button lets you change your mind about a recent purchase. It does not cancel something you have been paying for months.

What do I do in the meantime?

Cancel through whatever channel each company asks for, and keep proof. You'll find it company by company in Cancel a subscription, with the official source and the date it was checked.

What repeats on your account?

Drop your bank statement here and you'll see your subscriptions, the ones that have gone up and the ones you pay twice. It is read on your device, not uploaded.
